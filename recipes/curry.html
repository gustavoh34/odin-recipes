Curry Recipe

Description

Japanese curry (karē) is a thick, glossy, and deeply savory stew characterized by its velvety texture and mild, slightly sweet flavor profile. Built upon a foundational roux of flour, fat, and a mild curry powder blend, the sauce is simmered down with pureed aromatics like onions, apples, and carrots, which lend the dish its signature touch of natural sweetness.

Description

  • Curry Roux Blocks
  • Boneless Chicken Thighs
  • Onions
  • Potatoes
  • Carrots
  • Minced Garlic and grated fresh ginger
  • Water or Chicken Stock
  • Japanese Rice

Steps

  1. Sear the meat: Heat oil or butter in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add your bite-sized pieces of chicken, pork, or beef and cook until all sides are nicely browned. Take the meat out and set it aside.
  2. Caramelize the onions: Lower the heat to medium. Add the chopped onions to the same pot. Cook them down for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring frequently, until they turn soft and translucent. For a deeper flavor, cook them longer until they are a rich golden brown.
  3. Fragrance with aromatics: Toss in the minced garlic and grated fresh ginger. Sauté for about 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Simmer the vegetables and meat: Return the seared meat to the pot along with the carrot and potato chunks. Pour in the water or stock, add the grated apple and honey, and bring the liquid to a boil.
  5. Skim and cook: Once boiling, skim off any foam or scum that rises to the surface. Reduce the heat to low, cover the pot with a lid, and let it simmer for about 20 minutes, or until the potatoes and carrots are easily pierced with a fork.
  6. Dissolve the curry roux: Turn off the heat completely. Break your store-bought curry roux blocks into individual cubes and add them to the pot. Stir gently until the blocks are completely dissolved into the hot liquid without any lumps remaining.
  7. Thicken the sauce: Turn the heat back on to low. Let the curry simmer uncovered for another 5 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally to make sure the bottom doesn't burn, until the sauce thickens into a rich, glossy gravy.
  8. Serve: Ladle the hot curry generously over a bed of freshly cooked, sticky short-grain Japanese white rice.
