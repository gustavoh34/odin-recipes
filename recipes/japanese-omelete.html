Japanese Omelete Recipe

Description

A Japanese omelet, known as tamagoyaki (卵焼き), is a sweet and savory rolled egg dish characterized by its distinct rectangular shape and delicate, pillowy layers.

Ingredients

  • Eggs
  • Dashi
  • Mirin
  • Sugar
  • Soy Sauce
  • Salt
  • Cooking Oil

Steps

  1. Whisk the egg mixture: Crack the eggs into a bowl. Add the dashi, mirin, sugar, soy sauce, and salt. Whisk gently using a cutting motion with chopsticks or a fork to blend the whites and yolks without creating too many air bubbles or foam. For an extra smooth texture, pour the mixture through a fine-mesh strainer.
  2. Prep your oil station: Pour a small amount of neutral cooking oil into a small dish. Fold a paper towel into a small square, dip it into the oil, and use it to lightly grease your pan.
  3. Heat the pan: Place your rectangular tamagoyaki pan (or a small non-stick round skillet) over medium-low heat.
  4. Pour the first layer: Dip your paper towel back into the oil and wipe the pan to ensure it is evenly greased. Pour a thin layer of the egg mixture (about 1/4 cup) into the pan, tilting it to coat the bottom completely.
  5. Pop air bubbles: As the egg cooks and sets, quickly pop any large air bubbles that form with your chopsticks or spatula so the layers stay tight and uniform.
  6. Roll the first layer: When the egg is mostly set but still slightly wet on top, use your chopsticks or a flexible spatula to carefully roll the egg layer from the front of the pan back toward you until it forms a tight log at the handle side of the pan.
  7. Prep for the second layer: Slide the rolled egg log back to the far end of the pan. Use your oiled paper towel to grease the newly exposed empty surface of the pan.
  8. Pour the second layer: Pour another thin layer of the egg mixture into the empty space. Lift the existing egg log slightly with your chopsticks so the raw liquid egg flows completely underneath it. This binds the new layer to the old one.
  9. Roll the second layer: Once this new layer is mostly set but still soft, roll the original egg log forward over the new layer until it reaches the front of the pan again.
  10. Repeat the process: Slide the growing log back to the handle side, oil the empty pan, pour more egg mixture (letting it run underneath the log), and roll. Repeat these steps until all of your egg mixture is gone.
  11. Shape and cool: Remove the finished egg log from the pan. Wrap it immediately in a bamboo sushi mat (makisu) or parchment paper, pressing it gently to square off the edges and tighten the layers. Let it rest for 5 minutes to set its shape.
  12. Slice and serve: Unwrap the omelet and slice it into bite-sized cross-sections (about 1/2 to 1 inch thick) using a sharp knife to display the beautiful internal spiral layers.
