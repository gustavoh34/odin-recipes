Onigiri Recipe

Description

An onigiri (also known as omusubi) is a popular, handheld Japanese comfort food made of seasoned short-grain white rice compressed into a neat triangular, round, or cylindrical shape and typically wrapped in a crisp sheet of nori (dried seaweed).

Ingredients

  • Short-Grain Japanese Rice
  • Nori
  • Salt
  • Water
  • Salted Salmon

Steps

  1. Cook the rice: Wash short-grain Japanese rice thoroughly until the water runs clear. Cook it in a rice cooker or on the stove. Once done, let it steam covered for 10 minutes, then gently fluff it. Let it cool slightly until it is warm enough to handle comfortably but not cold.
  2. Prepare the salted salmon: Season a salmon fillet generously with sea salt on both sides. Grill, pan-fry, or bake the salmon until it is fully cooked and flaky. Remove the skin and any bones, then flake the flesh into small pieces with a fork and let it cool.
  3. Set up your workstation: Place a bowl of clean water, a small dish of sea salt, your cooked warm rice, your flaked salmon, and your cut nori sheets within easy reach.
  4. Wet and salt your hands: Dip both hands into the water bowl to wet your palms. This prevents the sticky rice from clinging to your skin. Rub a pinch of sea salt evenly across your wet palms to season the outside of the rice ball.
  5. Form the rice base: Scoop a handful of warm rice (about 1/2 cup) into one hand. Gently press your thumb into the center of the rice scoop to create a small indentation or pocket for the filling.
  6. Add the salmon filling: Place 1 to 2 teaspoons of the flaked salted salmon directly into the well you just created.
  7. Seal the filling: Gently fold the surrounding rice over the salmon pocket. Scoop a tiny bit of extra rice on top if needed to completely cover and conceal the salmon filling inside.
  8. Shape into a triangle: Cup your hands together. Use one hand to form a "V" shape angle over the top of the rice ball while your other hand supports the flat bottom. Gently squeeze and rotate the rice ball 3 times to form a neat, compact triangle. Do not squeeze too hard; the rice should hold together but remain light and fluffy.
  9. Wrap with nori: Place a strip of crisp nori seaweed at the bottom center of your triangle. Fold the seaweed upward over both sides of the rice ball. The natural moisture from the warm rice will help the seaweed stick perfectly.
  10. Garnish and serve: Place a tiny flake of salted salmon right on the exposed top peak of the triangle so you can easily identify what filling is hidden inside. Enjoy immediately while the seaweed is crisp!
